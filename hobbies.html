My Hobbies

Woodworking

Woodworking has been a hobby of mine for many years. There is something captivating about the grandeur and asymmetry found in nature that cannot be artificially recreated. When I build a project with the intention of capturing nature's flow, I tend to work the pieces with machinery as little as possible. Retaining nature's intent throughout the piece I'm designing feels humbling, as if we cannot improve upon the culmination of nature's aesthetics. I think we would all be better off decorating our homes with at least one small reminder of the beauty permeating our origin, a reminder to be grateful.

Woodworking can be dangerous. I once had a piece of wood I was sawing fling back at me despite having the guard fastened properly, and I've also had a blade on the log lathe shoot the wood chip of a knot through an aluminum can. Though I tend to be cautious I am not risk-averse, but nor am I reckless.

Systems Architecture

Complex mapping and diagramming is similar to the product of woodworking, in that it is a way for me to creatively design something that functionally reinvigorates a system. Because my method is unique, it is both dependent and independent of the context: the structure of the method is independent of the context, but the contents are dependent on context. In this way, regardless of what the context may be (whatever the subject of the system I am mapping) can be tackled efficiently and systematically. In a sense, it is a universal method of resolving frictions between the components.

The system also takes both inductive and deductive reasoning, and both supervenience and subvenience into consideration in its structure. In this way, it becomes simple to identify emergent properties and reductive properties. This becomes useful because it simplifies how to determine the magnitudes of effects between the tiers of the structure. Making correlation judgments and magnitudes of change judgments are valuable when determining what factor to change, and how much to change the factor to get the desired result.

Because my mind organizes the structure of data in this way naturally, it is effortless, and I have come to embrace, rather than resist, the flow of systems.

Swordsmanship

I have always loved swords and the cultivation that accompanies learning swordsmanship. In modern times, it is good to have a healthy dose of the archaic to balance out our expectations. Through practicing swordsmanship, I develop mind, body, and spirit in tandem during a single activity. Refining myself through discipline, physical fitness, and spiritual sensitivity, I discovered insights that lie concealed in the cavern of the self.

Training the mind, body and spirit has benefits to any person willing to grow. It results in a well-rounded individual—somebody capable in all realms—whose versatility can be adapted according to the situation. I think this is more useful than over-specializing in a single realm as this enables the individual to have the potential to dig deeply into any realm of subjects and not be bound to the constraints of a single subject.

Learning that one can in fact injure others leads them to question when it is appropriate and necessary to do so. This internal exploration assists in cultivating an ethical credo and standard of conduct to abide by in daily life. The insight to cultivate character, virtues, and to balance them accordingly when appropriate, should be a goal each person strives towards. I found there is a way to derive guidance from the self beyond mere anecdote, that the heart and mind of human beats and flows within each of us regardless of our experience.

This led me to an earnest desire to help others by focusing my systems mapping on resolving divisive, contentious issues. We are not here to benefit one or the other: we are here to help one another in ways that benefit, as equally as possible, all of us. This is the foundational starting point of my goal, duty, and desire.
